z 02 Orientation

Two ends of the same problem.

I am a Master of Computer Science student at UC Irvine, doing 3D cell-lineage reconstruction research on CellUniverse under Dr. Wayne Hayes. My day is spent making a C++ optimizer fit oblate spheroids to noisy confocal volumes — Monte Carlo search, PCA-based division detection, and enough numerical care that the results are worth putting in a manuscript.

The other half of my work is production software. At LaunchPath I built LaunchMail end-to-end — Spring Boot and React/TypeScript, RAG-grounded email generation over pgvector, autonomous LLM agents on a webhook-resumed Postgres state machine. Research taught me to be honest about what an algorithm actually does; shipping taught me that correctness only counts once it survives 242 integration tests.

Running underneath both is machine learning and computer vision, mostly in Python. I spent eighteen months at UC Santa Cruz's AIEA Lab on Gaussian splatting — reconstructing hazardous driving scenes so autonomous vehicles could be tested against them, alongside object-detection work in PyTorch and TensorFlow; the work on making those reconstructions fast enough to replay came out of that. More recently I trained PPO agents to play Doom, which turns out to be a cheerful way to learn how much of reinforcement learning is really reward design.

Cell tracking is the same problem wearing a lab coat: find the objects, follow them through time, and be honest about the ones you got wrong. I pick up new stacks quickly and I work well either way — inside a team or alone with a hard problem and a profiler.
